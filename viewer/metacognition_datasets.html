Metacognition datasets

Benchmarks that measure whether a model knows what it knows — the meta level MMLU does not touch. One of the four is fetchable.

Fetched — Metacognitive Monitoring Battery

arXiv:2604.15702 · chapter5/datasets/metacog_battery · 524 items over six domains, run on 30 models. Grounded in Nelson & Narens (1990). The repo ships per-model per-item results, so both the item structure and every model's behaviour are inspectable.

withdraw delta — the battery's headline metric: withdrawal rate on incorrect items minus withdrawal rate on correct items. A model that knows what it does not know withdraws more when wrong. Two behavioural channels are recorded per item: keep_withdraw (retract the answer?) and bet_nobet (wager on it?).

MIRROR not released

Wang 2026, arXiv:2604.19809. 5,000 domain-stratified questions, 8 cognitive domains, four levels — L0 atomic self-knowledge, L1 cross-domain transfer, L2 compositional prediction, L3 adaptive self-regulation. Original items, not MMLU. Five behavioural channels: wagering, opt-out, difficulty selection, tool delegation, natural-language signals.

Design note for us. MIRROR does not pre-ask the model to rank its own domains; proficiency is derived post-hoc from measured accuracy. Our top-3/worst-3 self-report is the elicited version, which they chose not to do.

“Code, data, and Croissant metadata will be released publicly” — no URL in the preprint.

The Metacognitive Probe not released

arXiv:2605.09844. Five tasks, 15 slots: confidence calibration, epistemic vigilance, knowledge boundary, calibration range, reasoning-chain validation. The knowledge-boundary dimension is our anachronism construct in generic form.

No repository link in the preprint.

LLMs Show No Signs Of Individuated Metacognition no dataset

Moran & Whiting, arXiv:2605.24299, May 2026. Six benchmarks including MMLU-Pro, 20 models, 8 labs.

“models retrieving facts share an item-level difficulty axis and differ mainly in their decision thresholds along it”

Confidence variation collapses to roughly one shared difficulty factor. Models agree which items are hard and differ only in where they set the threshold — so confidence is a difficulty estimate, not self-knowledge. Their bar for genuine self-knowledge: one model's confidence must predict its relative advantage over another model, controlling for base rates.

Consequence for the self-report design. Run the self-report arm on the uninduced model too and report the persona-minus-baseline difference. If persona self-reports differ beyond a threshold shift, that is the finding. If they do not, this paper predicted it.
